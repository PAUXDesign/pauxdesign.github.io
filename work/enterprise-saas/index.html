Overview

A law firm came to me with a real problem: their entire compliance operation lived in Excel. 850 rows. Manual reminders. Legal documents written for lawyers that non-legal staff had to somehow follow — without making a single regulatory mistake.

The ask was to turn that into a SaaS. But not just any SaaS — one that would cover GDPR, occupational health, HR processes and cross-departmental workflows, for multiple types of organisations, each with different roles, permissions and legal responsibilities. A product where getting it wrong had legal consequences.

I was brought in as Lead Product Designer and UX Architect. I owned the project end-to-end: reading the regulatory frameworks, translating them into product logic, defining the IA, designing all 10 modules, and working directly with stakeholders and engineering until it shipped.

The problem
"How do you translate 850 rows of Excel and dense legal documents — written for lawyers — into a system non-technical users can follow without ever making a compliance error?"
Before
  • 850+ rows of Excel tracking certifications manually
  • Legal documents written for lawyers, not end users
  • Manual email reminders for every deadline and approval
  • No visibility into who reviewed, approved or signed what
  • Audit processes undocumented and error-prone
The vision
  • Compliance enforced by structure — not user discipline
  • Legal logic translated into guided, step-by-step UI
  • Automated notifications based on real-time status
  • Full audit trail built into every action
  • Role-based access configured per organisation type
Platform dashboard — compliance status across all organisations

Design approach

Compliance couldn't be a warning layer. If users have to understand a regulation, interpret a warning and decide what to do next, the product is putting the complexity back on them.

The goal was to move that complexity into the system itself — turning requirements into workflows, permissions, validations and states wherever possible.

The goal wasn't to teach users the regulatory framework. It was to make the correct path clear through the product.

01
Turn requirements into product rules

Regulatory and business requirements were translated into workflow rules, validations, permissions and required actions instead of being left as information for users to interpret.

02
Make the system context-aware

Navigation, data and available actions change depending on the user's organisation, role and responsibilities, so users only see what is relevant to them.

03
Guide instead of educate

HR managers, legal assistants and operations staff shouldn't need to understand the whole regulatory process to complete a task. The product provides the right information and next action when they need it.

04
Design the system before the screens

The main challenge was understanding how 10 modules would share data, permissions, workflow states and audit history. Individual screens came after the underlying system made sense.

Key decisions
🏗️
System-first architecture

10 modules, one connected product. The platform needed to support very different areas without becoming a collection of separate tools. Shared rules for data, permissions, workflow states and audit history created consistency across the system while allowing each module to work for its own domain.

🔐
Roles as configurable containers

A job title couldn't define access on its own. The same role could have different responsibilities depending on the organisation or context. Instead of creating many fixed permission profiles, roles became configurable containers that organisations could adapt to their own structure.

⚙️
Guided compliance workflows

The system controls the process, not the user's memory. Documents, reviews, approvals and signatures move through predefined states. Required actors, conditions and available actions are built into the workflow, reducing the chance of skipping a required step or losing track of what happens next.

How workflows actually enforce compliance

The hardest part wasn't designing the flow. It was designing the rules behind it.

Each step is linked to the appropriate role. Actions become available only when the required conditions are met. Deadlines and pending actions trigger system-driven follow-up, while important actions are recorded with their actor, timestamp and state.

Instead of asking users to remember the process, the system guides them through it.

✅
Correct actors, every time. Each workflow step is tied to the right role, and the system only advances once the required conditions are met.
🔔
Automated follow-up replaces manual chasing. Deadlines and pending actions trigger system-driven reminders and status updates based on the latest state of the workflow.
🚫
Non-compliant paths do not exist. Users are not asked to interpret a warning or decide whether to proceed — the next action is simply unavailable until the required step is completed.
📋
Every action is auditable. Actors, timestamps and workflow states are recorded as part of the process, creating a structured trail of compliance activity.

Outcome & impact

The project transformed an Excel-based compliance operation into a structured SaaS platform with 10 specialised modules.

🔒
Manual tracking was replaced with guided workflows. The platform creates a clear process for each action and enforces the right steps, reducing the risk of missed approvals or incomplete records.
⚡
Permissions were simplified without losing control. Configurable roles gave organisations flexibility while keeping access logic structured and auditable.
📈
Compliance became part of the operating model. Status, states, approvals and deadlines became structured system behaviours rather than informal human memory.

Manual tracking was replaced with:

  • guided workflows
  • configurable permissions
  • structured document and approval states
  • automated status-driven actions
  • auditable activity history
  • organisation-level data separation

The result was a product where regulatory requirements became part of the workflow itself, reducing reliance on manual coordination, user memory and spreadsheet-based tracking.

Key learnings
📖
Regulatory complexity becomes a design problem. The value wasn't in memorising legislation. It was in understanding the requirements well enough to turn them into product behaviour — rules, workflows, permissions and constraints.
🔗
The hardest UX problems were architectural. Users don't see how 10 modules share data, permissions or audit history. But these relationships are what make the product work as one system.
🤝
Cross-functional alignment is a product design skill. Legal, business and engineering teams bring different requirements to the same problem. A big part of the design work was turning those different views into decisions the product could support.
🗂️
Multi-domain products need strong shared patterns. Ten modules meant different domains, workflows and mental models. The challenge was allowing each area to work for its users while keeping a consistent product underneath.
